### Per-item notes

**1. 2.1 App Completeness.** Exact text: "include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login." Alternative: "you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple." The "Before You Submit" checklist adds "Enable backend services so that they're live and accessible during review." ASC field reference says the demo "Account must not expire." Apple publishes **no** OTP-specific guidance; forum threads (SECONDARY) converge on: a test phone number whose OTP is a fixed server-side code, stated in Notes.

**2. 4.8 Login Services.** Trigger: using "a third-party or social login service … to set up or authenticate the user's primary account." Exemption bullet 1: "Your app exclusively uses your company's own account setup and sign-in systems." Phone+OTP against Seif's API is exactly that. No Sign in with Apple needed.

**3. 3.1.1 / 3.1.3 — the purchase-silent model.** Current text (post-1 May 2025):
- 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app … you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content … such as license keys …"
- 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform: access to items "acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site … provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app."
- 3.1.3(a) Reader apps limited to "magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video" (support/reader-apps/). Education/quiz content does not qualify.
- 3.1.3(c) Enterprise: only when "sold directly by you to organizations or groups"; "Consumer, single user, or family sales must use in-app purchase."
- 3.1.3(f) Free stand-alone companions: examples "VoIP, Cloud Storage, Email Services, Web Hosting"; requires "no purchasing inside the app, or calls to action for purchase outside of the app."
- 3.1.3 intro: apps "cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront."
- 3.1.1(a): "In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront … apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase."

Reading for Level Up: no guideline clause cleanly authorises a consumer education subscription sold only on the web and unlocked in-app with no IAP. Closest fit is 3.1.3(b), which requires an IAP equivalent, or 3.1.3(f), whose examples are infrastructure services. This is a product/business decision for the owner, not something the app can engineer around. What the app **can** guarantee: no price, no upgrade link, no "buy on our website" copy, and — on the Egypt storefront — no link to the website redemption page, because that is a "call to action that direct[s] customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase". The External Purchase Link entitlement is listed only for EU/EEA, South Korea, Russia, Brazil, Japan; Egypt is not listed; the US exemption is storefront-specific. Also: the account-deletion FAQ states linking out to the default browser "to sign in or register an account … is not appropriate, per App Store Review Guideline 4."

**4. 5.1.1(v) account deletion.** "you must also offer account deletion within the app." Support page: "only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient"; "If people need to visit a website to finish deleting their account, include a link directly to the page"; manual/slow deletion acceptable if the user is told; data the developer "isn't legally required to maintain" must go. Customer-service-only flows only for 5.1.1(ix) regulated industries (education is not listed). Re-authentication (e.g., OTP) before deletion explicitly allowed.

**5. Privacy nutrition label + manifests.** "Collect" = "transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you … to access it for a period longer than … the transmitted request." Optional-disclosure exemption needs all four conditions — registration data fails it. Likely rows, all "Linked to you", purpose App Functionality: Contact Info (Name, Phone Number, Email Address; parent phone also Phone Number), Identifiers (User ID; app-generated random device ID fits "Device ID"), User Content → Gameplay Content (quiz answers, leaderboard), Other Data (DOB, school/university/governorate/stage), Diagnostics none, Tracking: No. Manifest facts: "Starting May 1, 2024, apps that don't describe their use … aren't accepted"; each executable/dylib that uses a required-reason API needs its own manifest — a Runner `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` with `NSPrivacyTracking=false` and collected-data types is the accepted way to feed Xcode's privacy report. Category IDs: `NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults` (`CA92.1`), `…FileTimestamp` (`C617.1`), `…SystemBootTime` (`35F9.1`), `…DiskSpace` (`E174.1`), `…ActiveKeyboards` (`54BD.1`). SDK list hits: Flutter, connectivity_plus, device_info_plus, url_launcher(+`_ios`), sqflite, path_provider(+`_ios`), `shared_preferences_ios` (old name of `shared_preferences_foundation`; Apple: "any SDKs that repackage those on the list, are included"). Not listed: flutter_secure_storage, cached_network_image, flutter_bloc, riverpod, pinput, flutter_svg, country_flags, screen_protector. Firebase additions that ARE listed: FirebaseMessaging, FirebaseCore, FirebaseInstallations, GoogleUtilities, nanopb, Promises. Upload errors: ITMS-91053 (missing API reason), ITMS-91061 (listed SDK without manifest; 12 Feb 2025 enforcement), ITMS-90683 (missing purpose string).

**6. Screenshot blocking.** No guideline mentions screenshots. Apple DTS (forums/thread/824922, May 2026): using `isSecureTextEntry` as a container "is not its intended purpose", the behaviour is "a side effect … subject to change at any time without notice", citing 2.5.1. `screen_protector` 1.5.3 wraps ScreenProtectorKit, whose README states the `isSecureTextEntry` technique (SECONDARY; closed xcframework). Treat iOS screenshot blocking as a review risk with no documented benefit; Android FLAG_SECURE is unaffected.

**7. Age rating.** Tiers 4+/9+/13+/16+/18+, per country. Descriptors: "Contests" (infrequent → 4+, frequent → 13+) probably covers leaderboards; "Social media" = "redistribution, amplification, or interaction with user-generated content through a social feed" — a rank list is arguably not that; "Age assurance" and "Parental controls" are optional declarations. "If your app has a EULA with minimum age requirements that exceed the rating … you must override." Social-media questions: "beginning in September 2026, responses will be required when submitting new apps or updates."

**8. Age assurance laws.** Texas SB2420 in force for new Apple Accounts from 4 Jun 2026; Utah 6 May 2026; Louisiana 1 Jul 2026; Brazil age categories; 18+ apps blocked for unverified users in AU/BR/SG (Level Up is not 18+). Apple: "In those regions, you must check the age of the people using your app"; "there are no changes to the App Review process." The API is iOS 26.2+/Xcode 26.2+ native (no Flutter plugin from Apple). If the US storefront is excluded, nothing is required.

**9. Kids.** 5.1.4(b): apps "that collect, transmit, or have the capability to share personal information … from a minor must include a privacy policy and must comply with all applicable children's privacy statutes." Kids Category rules apply only if opted in. 5.1.4(a): "Apps may ask for birthdate and parental contact information only for the purpose of complying with these statutes" — the hook for keeping DOB/parent phone; the privacy policy should say so.

**10. Push (future Firebase).** 4.5.4 as in table. Adding FCM means APNs `.p8` key, Push capability + Background Modes → Remote notifications; FirebaseMessaging "Records the APNs token and associates it with a collected app installation ID" plus device model, language, time zone, OS version (SECONDARY: firebase.google.com) → add Identifiers/Device ID and diagnostics to the label; listed Firebase pods must carry manifests and signatures.

**11. Export compliance.** "the use of encryption that's built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using URLSession—is exempt." Keychain via flutter_secure_storage is OS encryption. `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption=NO` "streamlines the app submission process." French declaration only if distributing in France and only for non-OS algorithms.

**12. ASC metadata fields.** Support URL: required, "with contact information (legal address, email, phone)". Privacy Policy URL: required. App Review contact name/email/phone: required. Notes: up to 4000 bytes, "Can be in any language."

**13. Localization/RTL.** No rule that metadata languages match binary languages. Only 2.3 accuracy applies (Arabic screenshots must show real Arabic UI).

**14. Trader status.** "Even if you don't distribute apps in the EU, you'll still need to declare a trader status" — pick "This is not a trader account." when the EU is excluded.

**15. 2025–2026 guideline revisions (dated).** 1 May 2025: 3.1.1/3.1.1(a)/3.1.3/3.1.3(a) US-storefront link-outs. 9 Jun 2025: 3.2.2(x). 6 Feb 2026: 1.2 covers random/anonymous chat. 8 Jun 2026: Introduction "revised kid and teen safety guidance"; 1.2 developer responsibility paragraph; 4.3(a)/(b) spam examples; 4.5.3 Live Activities. Nothing in Jun–Sep 2026 mandates accessibility labels or changes privacy manifests.

**16. 3.2.2(v)** "Arbitrarily restricting who may use the app, such as by location or carrier" — if OTP only works for +20 numbers, say why in Notes.

### Ambiguities / could not confirm

- **Whether Apple accepts a web-only-subscription education app with no IAP at all.** Guideline text does not authorise it outside reader/enterprise/multiplatform-with-IAP; enforcement in practice is not documented by Apple. Owner decision.
- **Leaderboard display names as UGC.** Plausibly included; no Apple statement either way.
- **`screen_protector` iOS mechanism.** Closed xcframework; README sentence only. UNVERIFIED at source (github.com/prongbang/ScreenPreventerKit 404).
- **Minimum deployment target.** Apple sets no floor; Xcode 26 supports "on-device debugging in iOS 15 and later."
- **Annual US BIS self-classification report.** Not mentioned on any Apple page read.
- **Utah/Louisiana law text.** Only Apple's dates read.
- JS-rendered pages read via Apple's JSON backend or browser: privacy-manifest-files, describing-use-of-required-reason-api, itsappusesnonexemptencryption, complying-with-encryption-export-regulations, declaredagerange, supporting-associated-domains, uikit/requesting-access-to-protected-resources.
